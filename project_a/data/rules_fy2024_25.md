# Statutory Income Tax Rules — FY 2024-25 (AY 2025-26)

Governed by the **Income-tax Act, 1961** as amended by the **Finance Act, 2024** (enacted July 2024).
Scope: **Salaried Individuals in India** (Section 15 to 17).

---

## 1. Statutory Deductions from Salary (Both Regimes)

### Section 16(ia) — Standard Deduction
- **Old Regime:** Flat **₹50,000** or the actual gross salary, whichever is lower.
- **New Regime (§ 115BAC):** Raised to **₹75,000** by Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 (effective from AY 2025-26), or actual gross salary, whichever is lower.

---

## 2. Exemptions & Deductions Allowed Under Old Regime ONLY

### Section 10(13A) read with Rule 2A — House Rent Allowance (HRA) Exemption
Exempt amount is the **minimum of the following three**:
1. Actual HRA received from employer.
2. Actual rent paid minus 10% of Basic Salary (Salary for this purpose = Basic + DA forming part of retirement benefits).
3. 50% of Basic Salary if accommodation is located in a **Metro city** (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai); or **40% of Basic Salary** in any Non-Metro location.
*Note: If rent paid is $\le$ 10% of Basic Salary, HRA exemption is NIL.*

### Section 80C — Investments, Life Insurance, PF, ELSS, Home Loan Principal
- Maximum permissible deduction: **₹1,50,000** across all qualifying instruments.

### Section 80CCD(1B) — National Pension System (NPS) Additional Contribution
- Additional voluntary contribution to Tier-1 NPS: Up to **₹50,000** over and above the ₹1,50,000 limit under Section 80C.

### Section 80D — Medical Insurance Premium & Preventive Health Check-up
1. **Self, Spouse & Dependent Children:**
   - Maximum deduction: **₹25,000** (if self < 60 years).
   - Maximum deduction: **₹50,000** (if self $\ge$ 60 years, Senior Citizen).
   - Includes up to ₹5,000 for preventive health check-up (within overall cap).
2. **Parents:**
   - If parents are under 60 years: Additional up to **₹25,000**.
   - If parents are **Senior Citizens ($\ge 60$ years)**: Additional up to **₹50,000**.
   - Maximum combined 80D deduction possible (senior self + senior parents): **₹1,00,000**.

### Section 24(b) — Interest on Borrowed Capital for Self-Occupied House Property
- Maximum allowable deduction against salary income: **₹2,00,000**.

*Note: None of Sections 10(13A), 80C, 80CCD(1B), 80D, or 24(b) (self-occupied) are deductible under the New Tax Regime (§ 115BAC).*

## 4. Tax Rebate under Section 87A

### Old Regime
- **Eligibility:** Resident Individual whose Net Taxable Income does not exceed **₹5,00,000**.
- **Rebate Amount:** 100% of income tax payable or **₹12,500**, whichever is less.
- **Net Tax Payable:** ₹0 if Taxable Income $\le$ ₹5,00,000. No marginal relief exists under old regime for income exceeding ₹5,00,000 by even ₹1.

### New Regime (§ 115BAC)
- **Eligibility:** Resident Individual whose Net Taxable Income does not exceed **₹7,00,000**.
- **Full Rebate:** 100% of income tax or **₹25,000**, whichever is lower.
- **Section 87A Marginal Relief (New Regime Cliff Mitigator):**
  - If Net Taxable Income slightly exceeds ₹7,00,000, tax payable shall not exceed the income exceeding ₹7,00,000:
    $$\text{Tax Before Cess} \le (\text{Taxable Income} - ₹7,00,000)$$
  - Marginal relief rebate applies up to taxable income of **₹7,27,777**:
    - At ₹7,27,777: Slab tax is ₹20,000 + 10%(₹27,777) = ₹22,778. Excess income over ₹7,00,000 is ₹27,777. (Beyond this point, slab tax is lower than excess income).

## 5. Surcharge and Surcharge Marginal Relief

### Surcharge Rates
| Taxable Income Range | Old Regime Surcharge | New Regime Surcharge (§ 115BAC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Up to ₹50,00,000 | **0%** | **0%** |
| ₹50,00,001 to ₹1,00,00,000 | **10%** of tax | **10%** of tax |
| ₹1,00,00,001 to ₹2,00,00,000 | **15%** of tax | **15%** of tax |
| ₹2,00,00,001 to ₹5,00,00,000 | **25%** of tax | **25%** of tax |
| Above ₹5,00,00,000 | **37%** of tax | **25%** capped (Finance Act 2023 onwards) |

### Surcharge Marginal Relief
Surcharge is capped such that the total tax plus surcharge does not exceed:
$$\text{Tax on threshold limit} + (\text{Taxable Income} - \text{Threshold limit})$$
Where Threshold limits are ₹50,00,000; ₹1,00,00,000; ₹2,00,00,000; and ₹5,00,00,000.

## 6. Health and Education Cess

### Section 2(11) of Finance Act
- **Rate:** Flat **4%** applied on:
  $$\text{Cess} = 4\% \times (\text{Tax Before Cess} - \text{Rebate 87A} + \text{Surcharge})$$

---

## 7. Rounding Off Rules

### Section 288A — Rounding off Total Income
- Taxable income is rounded off to the nearest multiple of **₹10** (last digit 5 rounded up).

### Section 288B — Rounding off Tax Payable
- Total tax payable, rebate, surcharge, cess, and final tax are rounded off to the nearest multiple of **₹10** (last digit 5 rounded up).
